In May 2026, the research article “Manganese Pyrophosphate Mineralized DNA Nanovaccine Elicits Potent Humoral and Cellular Immunity,” coauthored by Shuangbo Dang, Ling Chen, and colleagues, was accepted and published online in *Angewandte Chemie International Edition*. Shuangbo Dang, Yun Sun, Ling Chen, and Yuhang Dong contributed equally to the work, with Professor Feng Li and colleagues serving as corresponding authors.

To address the insufficient immunogenicity of subunit vaccines, particularly their limited capacity to induce cellular immunity, our team collaborated with a research institute of China National Biotec Group. Rolling circle amplification was used to produce long DNA strands containing CpG and RBD aptamer sequences. Mn²⁺ was then mineralized with the pyrophosphate generated during amplification to form the manganese pyrophosphate mineralized DNA nanoadjuvant MnCA. After adsorption of the trimeric SARS-CoV-2 RBD antigen, the resulting MnCA+RBD nanovaccine was obtained. The central innovation goes beyond simply combining manganese and DNA: it integrates CpG–TLR9 signaling, Mn²⁺–cGAS–STING signaling, and nanoporous antigen delivery into a coordinated immunization platform that favors Th1-type cellular immunity and addresses a major limitation of conventional aluminum adjuvants.

The study provides new design principles for next-generation manganese-based adjuvants and subunit vaccines while demonstrating the potential of DNA nanoassembly for coordinated immune regulation and antigen delivery.

This work reflects the group’s continued exploration at the intersection of DNA nanoassembly, vaccine adjuvants, and biomedicine. Congratulations to Shuangbo Dang, Ling Chen, and all collaborators!
