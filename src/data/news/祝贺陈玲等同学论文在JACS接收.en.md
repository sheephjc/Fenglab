In July 2026, the research article “An orthogonal nanoadjuvant-based RSV subunit vaccine unlocks potent immunity through a high-entropy like immunostimulatory effect,” coauthored by Ling Chen and colleagues, was accepted for publication in the Journal of the American Chemical Society (JACS). The paper is not yet available online, and its publication details will be added after release.

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a major cause of lower respiratory tract infections, particularly among infants, children, and older adults. Developing a clinically acceptable and potent RSV vaccine has remained a long-standing challenge, with insufficient cellular immunity representing a key limitation of RSV F subunit vaccines.

The research team developed an orthogonal nanoadjuvant-based RSV subunit nanovaccine. The platform uses Mn/Al-layered double hydroxide (Mn/Al-LDH) nanosheets functionalized with the RSV F antigen and poly-CpG motifs. Within this system, aluminum promotes antibody-mediated humoral immunity, while manganese and CpG enhance cellular immunity by activating the cGAS–STING and Toll-like receptor pathways, respectively.

Coordinated activation of these pathways produces a “high-entropy-like” immunostimulatory effect that promotes the recruitment and activation of CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells, accelerates antigen-specific immunity, and supports durable T cell memory. The study also found an expanded CD134-positive subset among central memory T cells, suggesting enhanced capacity for clonal proliferation and differentiation into effector and memory populations, thereby preparing the immune system for a rapid and vigorous secondary response upon renewed antigen exposure.

In murine challenge models, the nanovaccine outperformed conventional aluminum-adjuvanted vaccines by significantly reducing viral load and alleviating lung pathology. The work establishes a new strategy for effective RSV vaccine development, with potential application to vaccines against other difficult pathogens.
